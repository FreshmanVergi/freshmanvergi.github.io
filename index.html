Personal x86 Compiler

Why it matters

I want to understand, end to end, how source code becomes machine-oriented instructions.

Goal

Design a custom programming language and build a compiler for it that targets the x86 architecture.

Approach

The intended pipeline covers lexical analysis, parsing, semantic analysis, an intermediate representation and x86 code generation. Each stage is documented as it is implemented and tested.

Where it stands

In development. No complete compiler, LLVM integration or optimisation passes are claimed yet.

Autonomous UAV Navigation and Maneuver Planning

Why it matters

An autonomous UAV has to know where surrounding objects are, and how far away, before it can choose a safe maneuver.

Goal

Under faculty supervision, investigate computational approaches for a single UAV to estimate its relative position and distance from surrounding objects, interpret spatial relationships and determine appropriate flight maneuvers.

Approach

Early-stage research and development on spatial perception, relative positioning, obstacle avoidance and motion planning, building on my UAV team experience.

Where it stands

Research in progress. No completed experiments, validated algorithms or flight tests are claimed. Supervisor: to be added after confirmation.

Compiler-Assisted Optimization for Heterogeneous Computing

Why it matters

Heterogeneous systems combine CPUs, GPUs and accelerators; where work runs changes both its latency and its energy use.

Goal

Explore compiler-assisted optimization for these environments, with a focus on execution latency, energy efficiency, workload scheduling and resource allocation.

Approach

Studying LLVM and MLIR as candidate frameworks for carrying performance-related information through optimization passes, and how static compiler decisions compare with runtime scheduling.

Where it stands

Early-stage: I have started the research and am studying the background, LLVM and MLIR. No implementation, benchmarks or findings yet.

Privacy-Preserving Decentralized Messaging

Why it matters

Most messaging depends on central servers; a decentralized design could shift both trust and routing to the participants.

Goal

Explore an architecture in which clients send encrypted messages and also act as relay nodes for others.

Approach

Investigating asynchronous communication, relay selection, privacy preservation and network performance under varying conditions, with latency, scalability and overhead as planned evaluation criteria in simulation.

Where it stands

Early-stage: I have started the research and am studying decentralized and privacy-preserving designs. Not experimentally validated; no simulations completed yet.

About

I am a Computer Engineering graduate from Yaşar University. My work so far spans embedded systems, aviation technologies, secure communication and mobile software, built for real users and real constraints.

Technology changes how people work and live, and that change is not always gentle. I take its disruptive side seriously, which is why I care about who can actually use what we build. In practice that comes down to four things I try to hold myself to.

Efficiency
Getting better performance and functionality out of the computational and physical resources that are already available.
Accessibility
Access to technology should be democratic. Advanced capabilities should not belong only to those with large budgets or large compute.
Affordability
Lowering the cost and resource requirements of a solution wherever it is practical to do so.
Real-world impact
Solving practical problems, rather than pursuing technical complexity for its own sake.
